Allergen rhythm

After each food has been introduced individually and tolerated, keep it in regular rotation. There is no exact medically required number of exposures per week.

🥜 Peanut

Monday, Thursday, Sunday (3× weekly)

Use smooth peanut butter thinned with water, breast milk, or tolerated yogurt. No thick spoonfuls.

🥚 Egg

Tuesday, Friday, and optionally Saturday (2–3× weekly)

Serve fully cooked, moist egg. Saturday pancakes with egg can count as a third exposure.

🥛 Dairy

Wednesday, Saturday, and optionally Sunday (2–3× weekly)

Plain, pasteurized full-fat yogurt. A spoonful alongside Sunday's meal can add a third exposure.

🌱 Other allergens

Fish, wheat, soy and sesame can be introduced one at a time when ready, then included regularly if tolerated.

If egg or dairy is new, introduce one at a time on a day you can observe Marley. Shift the calendar as needed. If she has severe eczema or prior reactions, discuss introduction with her pediatrician.

Making meals work

Iron first

Prioritize beef, lentils, beans, and iron-fortified infant oatmeal. Pair plant iron with vitamin-C foods like broccoli, kiwi, or strawberries.

Textures

Gradually move from smooth to thick mashed food and very soft finger foods as Marley is ready. Serve moist, easily squashed pieces; supervise upright in a high chair.

Breast milk

Continue breast milk or formula as the main nutrition source through the first year. Two solid-food opportunities are fine; let appetite guide portions.

Food storage

Cool and refrigerate promptly. For conservative home prep, use refrigerated meat-containing baby purées within about 24 hours and fruit/vegetable purées within about 48 hours, or freeze promptly. Thaw in the fridge and heat thoroughly before cooling to serve.

Safety

No honey before 12 months. Avoid whole nuts, whole grapes, hard raw vegetables, tough meat chunks, and thick nut butter. Cow's milk is not a replacement drink before age 1.

Supplements

Keep following the pediatrician's iron guidance. Since Marley has had hard stools, discuss ongoing constipation or supplement changes with her clinician. Check that she receives the recommended vitamin D.

This is a flexible planning aid, not a prescribed diet. New allergens should be introduced individually, and any concerning reaction needs medical attention; severe symptoms require emergency care.
